# Product Art Direction

Apply these rules to every new Myth Coloring product before writing its reviewed color plan.

- Treat the source image as the authority for the creature's recognizable form and main color traits.
- If the source background is a plain or nearly solid color, add a small, relevant black-line setting only when it helps the page read as a scene. Keep that setting visibly simpler than the creature and suitable to leave white.
- Preserve distinct, recognizable source colors in the flat Color Guide palette. Improve clarity, contrast, and printability without inventing extra colors solely to make an image look more colorful.
- Use flat, practical coloring-pencil or marker colors: no gradients, shading, glow, texture, or painted rendering in Color Guide or Finished Preview.
- The creature receives the complete recommended palette; background color is selective and optional. Every deliberately colored closed region must be fully filled in both Color Guide and Finished Preview.

## Color Scope Standard

For every product, assign color scope deliberately before building the reviewed region plan: color the creature and only objects visually tied to its pose or silhouette. Keep environmental ground, distant plants, small grass, and atmosphere consistently black-and-white unless the product brief explicitly includes them. Do not mix colored and uncolored background elements at random.
